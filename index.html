The land

A horse farm, reimagined for tents

Fern Gully was a working horse farm before it became a Hipcamp. Erin now shares the land with her boyfriend Dave and her mother Lauren, who both live on the property — her sister Morgan has since moved on to Texas, but the farm carries on.

Across the 15 acres, guests find quiet campsites folded into the forest and open pasture, plus a stargazing hammock circle for evenings when the sky clears over Puget Sound. The family spends most days outdoors — fixing fences, tending the fields, or simply lying in the hammocks — and they're always happy to point you toward the best blueberry stand in Freeland or a good night of live music at Mutiny Bay Blues.

What's in season

The farm ripens from July to September

Wild fruit lines the trails to every campsite — pick as you wander, and leave enough for the birds.

Blackberries

Thick along the fence lines by late summer — the reason many guests bring a container.

Cherries

Early-season fruit near the farmhouse and upper pasture.

Apples

Old farm trees still bearing fruit from the property's horse-farm days.

Salmonberries

Native berries in the wooded sites — sites 2 and 3 see them first.

Who you might meet
